Burst Pipe in Hollywood? Here’s Exactly What to Do

A pipe burst at 2 a.m. sounds like someone left a bathtub running inside your walls — because effectively, someone did. A single ruptured supply line can push out hundreds of gallons before morning, and most of it ends up exactly where you cannot see it: inside wall cavities, under cabinets, beneath flooring. Knowing what to do when a pipe bursts before it happens is worth more than any tool you own. Here is the sequence, in order.

What to Do When a Pipe Bursts: The First Five Minutes

  1. Shut off the water. Not the faucet — the supply. If you can reach the fixture's shutoff valve, turn it. If water is spraying and you cannot tell where from, go straight to the main shutoff and turn it clockwise until it stops. Every minute of flow is gallons.
  2. Kill the power to wet areas if you can reach the breaker panel without walking through water.
  3. Call for help now, not after you have "assessed." While you are mopping, water is wicking up drywall. Get emergency water extraction rolling while you handle the rest.
  4. Move valuables to high ground — electronics, documents, rugs, anything irreplaceable.
  5. Start documenting. Video the active leak if it is safe, then the spread. Narrate times out loud; you will forget them by morning.

Find Your Main Shutoff Today, Not During the Emergency

This is the single highest-value five minutes of homeownership, and almost nobody does it until water is already spraying. In most Hollywood homes the main shutoff is near the water meter by the street, in the garage where the line enters, or in a utility closet. Go look now. Turn it a quarter-turn to make sure it is not seized — older gate valves freeze up from disuse, which is a miserable discovery at midnight. If yours is stuck, a plumber can replace it with a quarter-turn ball valve in under an hour. While you are at it, show everyone in the household where it is. The person home during the burst is never the person who knows where the valve is.

The Electrical Check Everyone Skips

Water and electricity share walls, and supply lines love to run near wiring. If water is dripping from a ceiling fixture, running down a wall with outlets, or pooling near appliances, treat the area as live. Do not stand in water while touching anything plugged in. If the breaker panel is dry and reachable, kill the affected circuits. If the panel is wet or sitting in the flooded zone, leave it alone and call an electrician — breakers are not worth your life.

Burst Pipes in Florida Do Not Look Like Burst Pipes Up North

Up north, pipes burst because water froze and expanded. In Hollywood, they burst because they corroded, because water pressure ran too high for years, because the house still has original polybutylene piping from the eighties, or because slab movement stressed a copper joint one time too many. The practical difference matters: a freeze burst is usually one dramatic split, while a corrosion burst is often a pinhole that sprayed unnoticed inside a wall for hours. Check your water pressure — anything consistently above 80 psi is quietly stressing every joint in the house, and a ten-dollar gauge from the hardware store tells you in five minutes.

Contain It, Photograph It, Do Not Demolish It

Once the water stops, the instinct is to start tearing things out. Resist it. Your insurance adjuster needs to see the damage as it happened, and premature demolition can both weaken a claim and expose you to mold or contaminated insulation unnecessarily. Contain the spread with towels at doorways, photograph everything, and let the crew decide what gets cut. A burst pipe cleanup team maps the moisture first — and you would be surprised how often the wettest wall is not the one nearest the burst.

What Happens After the Water Stops

The plumber fixes the pipe; that is a different job from fixing what the water did. After the source is repaired, the remaining work is extraction of standing water, moisture mapping of every affected assembly, structural drying with monitored equipment, and documentation for your carrier. A supply-line burst starts as clean water, which is genuinely good news — most materials can be dried rather than discarded, but only if drying starts promptly. Frequently Asked Questions

How much water can a burst pipe release?

More than you would guess. A 3/4-inch supply line at normal pressure can release well over a hundred gallons an hour. An overnight burst can flood multiple rooms and saturate wall cavities a floor below. This is why the shutoff matters more than the mop.

My pipe burst inside a wall. How do I even find it?

Follow the water: stains, bulging paint, warm spots on the floor for hot lines, and the sound of running water when everything is off. A moisture meter or thermal camera finds it in minutes — one more reason to get professionals involved early instead of opening walls at random.

Will insurance cover a burst pipe?

Sudden bursts are typically covered; the slow leak you ignored for six months usually is not. The distinction is sudden versus gradual, and your documentation — photos, timelines, maintenance records — is what proves which one it was.

Should I call a plumber or a restoration company first?

Both, in this order: shut off the water yourself, call the restoration company to stop the damage from spreading, and get the plumber to fix the source. The pipe repair takes an hour; the water damage gets worse every hour you wait.

How do I prevent the next burst?

Check water pressure annually, replace aging rubber supply hoses with braided steel, know where your shutoff is, and if your home still has polybutylene piping, have a plumber evaluate it — that material has a well-earned reputation.
